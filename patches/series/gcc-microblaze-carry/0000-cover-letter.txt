microblaze: use the carry for shifts and overflow checks

MicroBlaze has a carry flag that add, addc, rsub, rsubc, srl, sra and
src set or read, but the port does not model it, and three common
operations are built from 32-bit pieces that ignore it.  Without a
barrel shifter, which the default multilib does not have, the results
are long:

	64-bit x << 1             36 instructions
	32-bit x >> 31            34
	64-bit add with overflow  19

counting the return and its delay slot.  This series adds patterns that
pass the bit through the carry:

1/3 shifts double words by one with add and addc, or srl or sra then
src.  In libgcc2.c's shift-and-subtract __udivmoddi4 the step that
doubles the remainder goes from 35 instructions to 2.

2/3 shifts words by 31 with add and addc, or srl and src, when there is
no barrel shifter.

3/3 adds uaddvdi4 and usubvdi4, which copy the carry out of the high
word into a register and branch on it.  There are no SImode patterns:
they made a check in longlong.h's udiv_qrnnd longer, and a 32-bit
compare is already one cmpu.

Each pattern keeps its two or three instructions together, since the
carry between them is not modelled; they are typed darith or multi, so
they stay out of delay slots.

Effect on libgcc's 64-bit division
----------------------------------

On today's libgcc the division routines do not change: __moddi3 is
moddi3.S, and the others do long division by 32-bit digits, which does
not use these shifts.  With the libgcc series posted separately
("libgcc: speed up no-divider modulo, drop moddi3.S"), MicroBlaze uses
libgcc2.c's shift-and-subtract code without a divider, and this series
speeds that up.

Estimated clock cycles per call, mean over 4701 operand pairs, on QEMU,
big-endian, with the libgcc series applied, before and after this
series.  "5-stage" and "3-stage" are MicroBlaze with C_AREA_OPTIMIZED=0
and 1:

	                  no options          barrel shifter,
	                                      hw multiply
	                  before    after     before    after
	5-stage
	  __moddi3         530.4    444.8      310.0    277.3
	  __umoddi3        392.6    334.0      220.7    200.7
	  __divdi3        1397.7    523.5      377.2    323.5
	  __udivdi3       1012.9    379.3      271.0    224.2
	3-stage
	  __moddi3         547.1    458.8      347.4    294.4
	  __umoddi3        403.5    357.3      246.8    226.3
	  __divdi3        1425.6    540.1      436.4    344.4
	  __udivdi3       1036.9    390.3      317.1    237.9

With the divider nothing changes.  The slowest single call with no
options, 5-stage, drops from 5527 to 1480 cycles for __divdi3 and from
5601 to 1484 for __udivdi3; today's long division takes up to 4047 and
4021.

The cycle counts are estimates.  A QEMU plugin counts the instructions
each call executes and costs them with UG984's per-instruction
latencies: 1 cycle for most, 34 or 35 for idiv, 1 or 3 for mul, 1 or 2
for barrel shifts, loads and stores, and for a branch 1 cycle not
taken, 2 taken with a delay slot and 3 without.  It assumes
single-cycle local memory, no branch target cache and no data hazard
stalls, which UG984 does not quantify.  I have not measured cycles on
hardware.  On a hand-counted test loop the plugin's counts were exact.

Code size
---------

In the default multilib (-mcpu=v4.00.a, no barrel shifter), built with
the libgcc series in both cases, the text of libc.a shrinks from 455825
to 420801 bytes (7.7%), libm.a from 233072 to 224816 (3.5%) and
libgcc.a from 84136 to 75888 (9.8%).

One case gets longer.  Double words live in even register pairs
(microblaze_hard_regno_mode_ok), but the ABI passes them in r5:r6 and
returns them in r3:r4, so a function that does nothing but shift its
64-bit argument by one now copies it in and out.  With a barrel shifter
that function goes from 5 instructions to 7; without one it stays at 7.
In a loop the value stays in its pair and the shift is two instructions.

Testing
-------

Tested on qemu-system-microblaze and qemu-system-microblazeel, machine
petalogix-s3adsp1800, gcc master df809a029 with the libgcc series and
this series, libgcc and newlib rebuilt with the patched compiler.  For
each byte order:

	gcc.c-torture/execute      24536 pass, 14 fail, 176 unsupported
	builtin-arith-overflow*      264 pass
	new gcc.target/microblaze     33 pass

The 14 failures are the same as without this series (fprintf-2.c needs
a filesystem; pr117432.c is a separate variadic-argument bug).  The
other gcc.target/microblaze failures, in fcmp1-4.c, nofcmp.c,
vanilla.c and picdtr.c, are front-end errors in those tests (implicit
function declarations, and a redefinition), the same without this
series.  I tested the series as a whole, not each patch on its own.
